Trade-policy shocks are a recurring source of risk in agricultural commodity markets, but their transmission into futures prices is rarely symmetric. This paper uses the 2025 U.S.–China trade friction as a setting in which tariff news arrived in discrete, well-identified announcements, and asks whether soybean futures responded differently to escalation than to de-escalation.

The analysis documents abnormal asymmetric behaviour around tariff events and characterises how quickly that asymmetry decays, with implications for hedging and margin-setting in agricultural futures.
